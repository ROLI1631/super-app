# 13. Data Lifecycle

## Purpose

This document defines the platform’s information lifecycle philosophy. It distinguishes between temporary information, permanent legal records, and future archival needs while preserving privacy and governance.

## Core Principles

- Information must be classified according to its role and retention need.
- Temporary information must be minimized and retained only for as long as necessary.
- Permanent legal records must remain immutable and protected.
- Future archival practices must preserve continuity without compromising privacy.
- The platform must preserve privacy discipline throughout the lifecycle of information.

## Temporary Information

Temporary information includes operational and analytical data that serves short-term platform functions. It should be retained only as long as it remains necessary and should not be treated as permanent evidence.

## Permanent Legal Records

Permanent legal records are preserved because they serve legal, evidentiary, or governance purposes. They must remain stable, protected, and clearly distinguished from temporary information.

## Future Archive

Future archival strategy must preserve essential continuity while keeping the platform’s privacy and governance obligations intact. Archival structures must remain conceptually separate from operational systems.

## Retention Philosophy

Retention should be purposeful. The platform should preserve information only when justified by legal, technical, or governance need.

## Privacy Philosophy

Privacy remains essential at every stage of the lifecycle. Data minimization, separation of purpose, and controlled retention are foundational to the platform’s integrity.

## Future Expansion

As the platform grows, the lifecycle model must remain adaptable without collapsing into uncontrolled retention. New modules must honor the distinction between temporary and permanent information.
