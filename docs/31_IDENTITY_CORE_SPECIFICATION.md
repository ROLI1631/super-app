# 31. Identity Core Specification

## Purpose

This document defines the Identity Core of the SO8FIREF platform. Identity Core is the first permanent core layer and the foundation of every future module. It establishes the architectural role of identity as the origin of continuity, ownership, participation, trust, and governance across the ecosystem.

## Core Principles

- Identity Core is the foundation of the platform.
- Every platform object begins from identity.
- Identity must remain coherent across all modules and layers.
- Ownership must remain with the identity owner.
- Privacy and trust must be preserved through the identity lifecycle.
- Identity Core must remain modular and extensible.

## 4. Identity Lifecycle

### Purpose

To describe the conceptual lifecycle of identity within the platform.

### Responsibilities

- Registration: the point at which an identity enters the platform’s recognized ecosystem.
- Activation: the phase in which the identity becomes active within the platform’s governance model.
- Authentication: the ongoing recognition of identity for participation and access.
- Update: the modification of identity attributes or standing within the platform.
- Suspension: the temporary restriction of identity activity when required by governance.
- Deletion: the controlled retirement of identity representation.
- Inheritance: the conceptual transfer of digital continuity and access responsibility.
- Historical Record: the preservation of identity history in a governed and non-reversible form.

## 6. Security Principles

### Purpose

To describe the architectural security principles that govern Identity Core.

### Responsibilities

- Privacy First: identity must remain protected from unnecessary exposure.
- Owner Control: the identity owner must retain authority over their identity-related rights.
- Recovery Key Ownership: the recovery capability must remain under the owner’s control.
- Passkey Philosophy: identity recognition must remain consistent with the platform’s constitutional model.
- Operator Responsibility: the operator provides infrastructure and governance without replacing the identity owner’s role.
- User Responsibility: the user remains responsible for personal stewardship of identity continuity.
