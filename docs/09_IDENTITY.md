# 09. Identity

## Purpose

This document describes the platform’s identity philosophy. It defines identity as a foundational architectural layer that supports ownership, trust, continuity, and governed participation across the ecosystem.

## Core Principles

- Digital identity is a structured representation of a person, organization, or entity within the platform.
- Ownership is a central concept and must remain under the control of the identity owner.
- Authentication must preserve security without violating the Constitution.
- Recovery must remain user-controlled and not dependent on platform-mediated restoration.
- Identity must be treated as a lifecycle concept that evolves over time.

## Digital Identity

Digital identity is not merely an account. It is the platform’s recognized representation of an entity’s presence, rights, and continuity. It supports participation across modules while preserving boundaries and governance.

## Ownership

Ownership is foundational. The platform must preserve the user’s control over their identity, credentials, and digital rights. The operator provides infrastructure, not ownership substitution.

## Authentication Philosophy

Authentication is treated as a trust mechanism. It must be secure, clear, and aligned with the platform’s constitutional model. It must not rely on unsupported or invasive methods.

## Recovery Philosophy

Recovery is a matter of user sovereignty. The platform must not assume the role of custodian of access. The user’s recovery capability remains an independent responsibility.

## Identity Lifecycle

Identity evolves through creation, use, continuity, and possible change over time. The architecture must preserve stable identity across future modules and services without collapsing identity into a narrow account concept.

## Future Expansion

Identity will expand as the platform grows into broader forms of participation, organization, and governance. New identity-related capabilities must strengthen continuity and trust rather than fragment the model.
