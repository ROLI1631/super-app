# 27. Identity System

## Purpose

This document defines the conceptual identity system of the SO8FIREF platform. Identity is treated as the foundation of the entire ecosystem because it governs continuity, ownership, participation, trust, legal recognition, and the relationship between users and the platform.

## Core Principles

- Identity is the foundation of the platform.
- Identity must remain coherent across all modules and domains.
- Ownership must remain under the user’s control.
- Privacy must be preserved throughout the identity lifecycle.
- The platform must remain a technical operator and not assume ownership of user identity.
- Identity must support trust, legal clarity, and future expansion.

## 1. Identity Philosophy

### Purpose

To define the role of identity as the organizing principle of the platform.

### Responsibilities

- Digital Identity: provide the platform’s recognized representation of a person, organization, or entity.
- Ownership: preserve the user’s authority over their identity, rights, and access relationships.
- Privacy: ensure that identity is represented without unnecessary exposure of sensitive information.
- Operator Responsibility: provide infrastructure and governance while remaining outside the user’s legal relationships.
- User Responsibility: preserve the personal stewardship of access and recovery capability.

## 2. Identity Lifecycle

### Purpose

To describe the conceptual stages through which identity exists and evolves inside the platform.

### Responsibilities

- Registration: establish a recognized identity within the ecosystem.
- Activation: confirm that the identity becomes active within the platform’s governance model.
- Authentication: affirm the identity’s current presence and rightful access.
- Usage: allow the identity to participate in modules and relationships.
- Suspension: limit or pause identity activity when required by governance or legal conditions.
- Deletion: retire identity representation in a controlled and lawful manner.
- Inheritance Concept: preserve continuity of digital rights through user-controlled transfer of access responsibility.

## 3. Identity Components

### Purpose

To describe the conceptual components that make up the identity system.

### Responsibilities

- Identity Profile: the structured representation of the identity’s presence and role within the platform.
- Passkey: the constitutional authentication element associated with the identity model.
- Recovery Key: the user-controlled recovery mechanism tied to identity continuity.
- Legal Consents: the recorded legal acknowledgements that support the identity’s participation.
- Trust Profile: the accumulated trust-related context associated with the identity.
- Verification Status: the recognized standing of the identity within the trust governance model.
- Communication Identity: the identity’s presence within the internal communication framework.
- Preferences: the identity’s configured relation to platform behavior and experience.
- Future Identity Extensions: reserved conceptual areas for future identity-based capabilities.

## 4. Identity Relationships

### Purpose

To explain how the identity system connects with the broader platform architecture.

### Responsibilities

- Trust: identity provides the basis for trust and trust evaluation.
- Legal: identity supports legal consent, accountability, and recognition of responsibility.
- Communication: identity governs how a person or organization appears within internal communication flows.
- Marketplace: identity supports participation, recognition, and trust in exchange-related contexts.
- Search: identity helps make people, organizations, and entities discoverable in a governed way.
- AI: identity provides the contextual basis for intelligent assistance and governed participation.
- Organizations: identity supports organizational representation and collective participation.
- Administration: identity supports oversight, governance, and platform stewardship.
