# 11. Privacy

## Purpose

This document defines the privacy philosophy of the platform. Privacy is treated as a foundational design concern that governs how information is collected, preserved, exposed, and retired.

## Core Principles

- The platform must minimize storage.
- The platform must minimize exposure.
- The user must retain ownership over their personal information where possible.
- Data must remain isolated according to purpose and context.
- Privacy must be designed into the architecture rather than added later.
- Privacy must be preserved through the entire information lifecycle.

## Minimal Storage

The platform should avoid unnecessary retention of information. Temporary and operational data must not be preserved longer than required for its purpose.

## Minimal Exposure

Exposure of information must be limited to what is necessary for the task or relationship at hand. The platform must avoid broad or unnecessary disclosure.

## User Ownership

Users retain ownership of their personally meaningful information and digital rights. The platform provides infrastructure and governance, not ownership substitution.

## Data Isolation

Different categories of information must remain appropriately separated. This reduces unintended correlation and preserves privacy boundaries.

## Privacy by Design

Privacy is a structural requirement. It must be considered from the outset of any architecture, governance, or capability expansion.

## Privacy Lifecycle

Privacy must be preserved from the point of creation through use, retention, archival, and eventual retirement. The platform must not lose privacy discipline at any stage.

## Future Expansion

As the platform broadens into additional modules and forms of participation, privacy controls must remain rigorous and adaptable. New capabilities must not weaken the privacy model.
